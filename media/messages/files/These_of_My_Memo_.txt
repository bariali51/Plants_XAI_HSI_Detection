Nutrient Deficiency Mapping in Plants Using Hyperspectral Imaging and Machine Learning
الوصف:
Plants need essential nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and magnesium to grow healthily and produce good yields. However, many farmers cannot easily detect when crops are lacking in these nutrients until it is too late, often when visible symptoms like yellowing leaves or stunted growth appear. These visible signs usually show up only when the plant has already been affected seriously. Traditional methods for checking nutrient levels—like laboratory testing of soil or leaves—are often slow, costly, and not suitable for large-scale monitoring. Hyperspectral imaging (HSI) provides a promising solution to this problem. It captures detailed information about how plants reflect light across many wavelengths, far beyond what the human eye can see. Each type of nutrient stress causes specific changes in the plant’s internal structure and pigments, which are reflected in its spectral signature. By analyzing this spectral data, it becomes possible to detect early signs of nutrient deficiencies even before any visible changes appear on the leaves. In this project, the goal is to develop a system that uses hyperspectral images to detect and classify nutrient deficiencies in plants. You will use machine learning models—such as support vector machines (SVM), decision trees, or deep learning networks—to recognize patterns in the spectral data that correspond to specific nutrient problems. The process includes several steps: first, preprocessing the hyperspectral data (for noise removal and normalization), then selecting the most informative wavelength bands, and finally training and evaluating the classification models. A strong part of the project is its potential use in precision agriculture. With accurate detection of nutrient stress, farmers can apply fertilizers more efficiently—only when and where they are needed. This not only saves cost but also reduces environmental harm caused by overuse of fertilizers.
